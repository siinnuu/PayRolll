1. Project Overview & Problem Statement

1.1 Problem Statement

In enterprise human resource management, manual and spreadsheet-based payroll calculation introduces critical business risks, including:

  • Data Redundancy & Anomalies: Duplicate employee master records leading to ghost employees and inconsistent demographic information.
  • Arithmetic Miscalculations: Manual tracking of daily rates, prorated unpaid leave deductions, and statutory tax/provident fund withholdings.
  • Duplicate Disbursements: Inability to prevent duplicate salary slips generated for the same employee in the same fiscal month.
  • Audit Trail Deficiencies: Lack of atomic transactional commits and referential integrity, making it possible to delete employee master records whose payroll or payment history remains active.

1.2 System Objectives

This system establishes a hardened, normalized relational DBMS architecture to enforce mathematical rigor, ACID transaction consistency, and enterprise auditability:

Data Integrity
Foreign key cascades (ON DELETE RESTRICT), check constraints, and unique compound keys guarantee zero orphan records.
Atomic Transactions
Multi-statement payroll generation runs inside isolated database transactions (START TRANSACTION / COMMIT / ROLLBACK).
3NF Normalization
Zero transitive or partial dependencies. Compensation settings, monthly attendance, and payment disbursement are decomposed cleanly.

5. Entity Relationships & Referential Integrity

Parent Entity Child Entity Cardinality Foreign Key ON DELETE Action ON UPDATE Action Business Rationale
employees salary_settings 1 : 1 employee_id RESTRICT CASCADE Enforced by UNIQUE(employee_id). Each employee has exactly one active pay structure. Cannot delete an employee if salary settings exist.
employees attendance 1 : N employee_id RESTRICT CASCADE One employee logs multiple monthly summaries over time. Restricted to prevent deletion of employees with attendance records.
employees payroll 1 : N employee_id RESTRICT CASCADE Composite constraint UNIQUE(employee_id, year, month) enforces at most 1 payroll per month per employee. Restricts master employee deletion.
payroll payments 1 : N payroll_id RESTRICT CASCADE A payroll statement can receive payment disbursements. Cannot delete payroll statements once payments have been issued.
users employees 0/1 : 1 user_id SET NULL CASCADE De-provisioning a system login user retains the employee profile while nullifying credentials.
Why ON DELETE RESTRICT matters in Payroll: Under financial regulations (SOX, statutory tax laws), payroll ledgers and disbursement receipts must never be deleted cascade-style. Deleting an employee master record is rejected if historical payroll, attendance, or payment records exist.

6. Normalization Analysis & Decomposition Proofs

Database normalization minimizes data redundancy and avoids insertion, update, and deletion anomalies. The schema is rigorously proved up to Third Normal Form (3NF).

First Normal Form (1NF) Atomic Attributes & Primary Key Existence
  • Condition: Every attribute contains only atomic (indivisible) values, and each record has a unique identifier without repeating groups or arrays.
  • Verification in Schema:
    • Employee names are decomposed into separate atomic columns: first_name and last_name.
    • Allowances are structured as explicit numeric columns (hra, transport_allowance, medical_allowance) rather than comma-separated strings or JSON arrays.
    • Every table defines an explicit integer surrogate primary key with AUTO_INCREMENT.
Second Normal Form (2NF) Removal of Partial Functional Dependencies
  • Condition: The relation is in 1NF and every non-prime attribute is fully functionally dependent on every candidate key (no partial key dependencies).
  • Verification in Schema:
    • In attendance, the candidate business key is (employee_id, year, month). Non-prime attributes such as present_days, paid_leaves, and unpaid_leaves depend strictly on the complete composite tuple — they do not depend solely on employee_id or solely on (year, month).
    • Employee demographic attributes (e.g. department, designation) are NOT duplicated in attendance or payroll. Storing department in payroll would cause a partial dependency on employee_id. Instead, foreign keys are linked.
Third Normal Form (3NF) Elimination of Transitive Dependencies
  • Condition: The relation is in 2NF and no non-prime attribute is transitively dependent on the primary key ($X \rightarrow Y$ where $Y \rightarrow Z$).
  • Verification & Snapshots:
    • In employees, designations and departments do not determine compensation rates directly. Compensation rules are isolated in salary_settings.
    • Financial Historical Snapshot Rationale: In payroll, storing basic_pay, allowances_total, and net_pay might seem like stored derived values. In relational theory and accounting compliance, this is temporal snapshotting, NOT a transitive dependency. If an employee's salary changes in November, the historical October payroll statement must remain immutably fixed at October's pay rate, proving true 3NF temporal stability.

8.1 Comprehensive Payroll Ledger with Outstanding Payment Reconciliation

Joins payroll, employees, and payments to compute disbursed vs. pending balances per payroll line:

8.2 Attendance-to-Payroll Verification Cross-Check Join

Validates that recorded payroll leave deductions exactly reflect unpaid leaves from attendance records:

SELECT e.employee_code, CONCAT(e.first_name, ' ', e.last_name) AS employee_name, a.total_working_days, a.present_days, a.unpaid_leaves, p.basic_pay, ROUND((p.basic_pay / a.total_working_days) * a.unpaid_leaves, 2) AS expected_leave_deduction, p.leave_deduction AS recorded_leave_deduction, CASE WHEN ROUND((p.basic_pay / a.total_working_days) * a.unpaid_leaves, 2) = p.leave_deduction THEN 'RECONCILED' ELSE 'VARIANCE_DETECTED' END AS audit_status FROM payroll p INNER JOIN employees e ON p.employee_id = e.id INNER JOIN attendance a ON p.employee_id = a.employee_id AND p.year = a.year AND p.month = a.month WHERE p.year = %s AND p.month = %s;

9. Aggregations, GROUP BY & Conditional Metrics

Demonstrating SUM(), AVG(), COUNT(), MIN(), MAX(), COALESCE(), and CASE WHEN conditional aggregation.

9.1 Department-Level Workforce & Salary Commitment Aggregation

SELECT e.department, COUNT(e.id) AS total_employees, COUNT(CASE WHEN e.status = 'ACTIVE' THEN 1 END) AS active_employees, COALESCE(SUM(s.base_salary), 0.00) AS total_base_payroll, COALESCE(AVG(s.base_salary), 0.00) AS avg_base_salary, COALESCE(MIN(s.base_salary), 0.00) AS min_base_salary, COALESCE(MAX(s.base_salary), 0.00) AS max_base_salary FROM employees e LEFT JOIN salary_settings s ON e.id = s.employee_id GROUP BY e.department HAVING COUNT(e.id) > 0 ORDER BY total_base_payroll DESC;

9.2 Payment Method Distribution with Pivot Aggregations

SELECT pm.payment_method, COUNT(pm.id) AS transaction_count, COALESCE(SUM(pm.amount_paid), 0.00) AS total_amount, COALESCE(AVG(pm.amount_paid), 0.00) AS avg_transaction_size, ROUND( (SUM(pm.amount_paid) / ( SELECT SUM(amount_paid) FROM payments WHERE status = 'SUCCESSFUL' )) * 100, 2 ) AS volume_share_percentage FROM payments pm WHERE pm.status = 'SUCCESSFUL' GROUP BY pm.payment_method ORDER BY total_amount DESC;

10. Monthly Salary Calculation Model & SQL Implementation

10.1 Mathematical Formal Formulae

Daily Salary Rate ($R_D$)
R_D = Basic Salary / Total Working Days
Unpaid Leave Deduction ($D_L$)
D_L = R_D × Unpaid Leave Days
Overtime Payment ($P_{OT}$)
P_{OT} = Overtime Hours × Overtime Hourly Rate
Gross Salary ($S_{Gross}$)
S_{Gross} = Basic + Allowances + P_{OT}
Total Deductions ($D_{Total}$)
D_{Total} = D_L + Tax + PF + Other Deductions
Net Disbursable Salary ($S_{Net}$)
S_{Net} = S_{Gross} - D_{Total}

10.2 Pure SQL Equivalent Calculation Query

This analytical query reproduces the exact Python business logic directly within the MySQL database engine:

SELECT e.id AS employee_id, e.employee_code, s.base_salary AS basic_pay, (s.hra + s.transport_allowance + s.medical_allowance + s.other_allowances) AS allowances_total, ROUND(a.overtime_hours * COALESCE( CASE WHEN a.overtime_rate > 0 THEN a.overtime_rate ELSE s.hourly_rate END, 0.00 ), 2) AS overtime_pay, -- Gross Pay ROUND( s.base_salary + (s.hra + s.transport_allowance + s.medical_allowance + s.other_allowances) + (a.overtime_hours * COALESCE( CASE WHEN a.overtime_rate > 0 THEN a.overtime_rate ELSE s.hourly_rate END, 0.00 )), 2 ) AS calculated_gross_pay, -- Leave Deduction ROUND((s.base_salary / a.total_working_days) * a.unpaid_leaves, 2) AS leave_deduction, -- Total Deductions ROUND( ((s.base_salary / a.total_working_days) * a.unpaid_leaves) + (s.base_salary * (s.tax_deduction_rate / 100.0)) + (s.base_salary * (s.pf_deduction_rate / 100.0)) + s.other_deductions, 2 ) AS calculated_total_deductions, -- Net Pay ROUND( (s.base_salary + (s.hra + s.transport_allowance + s.medical_allowance + s.other_allowances) + (a.overtime_hours * COALESCE( CASE WHEN a.overtime_rate > 0 THEN a.overtime_rate ELSE s.hourly_rate END, 0.00 ))) - (((s.base_salary / a.total_working_days) * a.unpaid_leaves) + (s.base_salary * (s.tax_deduction_rate / 100.0)) + (s.base_salary * (s.pf_deduction_rate / 100.0)) + s.other_deductions), 2 ) AS calculated_net_pay FROM employees e INNER JOIN salary_settings s ON e.id = s.employee_id INNER JOIN attendance a ON e.id = a.employee_id WHERE a.year = %s AND a.month = %s AND e.status = 'ACTIVE';

11. Transaction Architecture & ACID Guarantees

Financial ledger mutations must adhere to rigorous ACID guarantees. In the Payroll and Payment modules, transactions are orchestrated via database connection transaction contexts:

Atomicity ("All or Nothing")
When recording a payment, the creation of the payments record and the status change of the payroll statement to 'PAID' occur inside the same transaction block. If either statement fails, the entire transaction issues a ROLLBACK, leaving no partial state.
Consistency (Invariant Preservation)
Database constraints enforce validity at the storage layer: CHK_attendance_month (1..12), uq_payroll_employee_month_year (prohibiting duplicate monthly generation), and non-negative decimal checks.
Isolation (Repeatable Read)
InnoDB executes under REPEATABLE READ isolation level using multi-version concurrency control (MVCC). Read queries see a consistent snapshot without dirty reads ($G_1$) or non-repeatable reads ($G_2$).
Durability (Write-Ahead Logging)
Committed transactions write to the InnoDB redo log buffer and are flushed to disk (ib_logfile) before acknowledging success, guaranteeing survival across process restarts.
